Title: Fragmented Truth

Medium: Charcoal & Graphite
Artist: Samuel Matthew (GSM)

There is a moment,quiet, almost invisible,when a person realizes that the face they present to the world is not the one they truly carry within. Fragmented Truth lives inside that moment. ... See more This piece explores the tension between identity and illusion, between what is seen and what is hidden. The fractured surface reveals more than it conceals: beneath the polished exterior lies a vulnerable, searching self,one that cannot fully emerge, yet refuses to remain buried. The hands that pull away the outer layer do not belong to another,they are extensions of the same being, caught in an intimate struggle between acceptance and exposure. The cracks that run across the surface are not merely signs of damage, but records of experience,of pressure, silence, expectation, and survival. They speak of the quiet battles fought within the mind and soul, where truth is often suppressed to maintain the comfort of appearances. In this work, beauty and discomfort coexist. The revealed face is soft, almost serene, yet surrounded by tension,suggesting that truth, while liberating, is never without consequence. To uncover oneself is to risk vulnerability, to confront the parts long hidden, and to stand exposed in a world that often prefers illusion. Fragmented Truth is not just a portrait,it is a reflection of the human condition. It asks a simple yet unsettling question: How much of who we are is real, and how much is carefully constructed to be accepted? Through charcoal’s raw depth and the delicate transitions of tone, the piece becomes a visual confession,one that invites the viewer not just to observe, but to confront their own hidden fractures..

Title: Crown of Legacy

Medium: Charcoal pencil and colour
Artist: Samuel Matthew (GSM)

There are faces that exist in time, and there are faces that carry time within them. Crown of Legacy is not merely a portrait of a king; it is a meditation on heritage, authority, and the invisible weight of leadership. ... See more In this work, the subject stands not only as a ruler but also as a vessel of history. His gaze is steady, yet layered—holding both the pride of lineage and the burden of continuity. The crown he wears is more than an ornament; it is a symbol shaped by generations, carrying stories of triumph, sacrifice, resilience, and responsibility. The textures that surround him,patterns, symbols, and subtle inscriptions are echoes of culture, identity, and memory. They remind us that a king is never alone; he stands on the foundation of those who came before him, and his presence shapes those yet to come. The inclusion of colour within the monochrome composition is deliberate. It breaks through the stillness like a pulse,suggesting that tradition is not static but alive. It breathes, evolves, and continues to find relevance in a changing world. Yet beneath the regality lies humanity. The softness in his expression reveals a man beyond the title,a figure who must navigate not only power but expectation, duty, and the quiet sacrifices that leadership demands. In this tension between strength and vulnerability, the true essence of kingship is revealed. Crown of Legacy invites the viewer to reflect on what it means to inherit, to represent, and to endure. It asks us to consider that behind every symbol of power is a human story,complex, emotional, and deeply rooted in the past.

Size: 12" x 16"

Title: Where Love Lives

Medium: Charcoal pencil on paper
Artist: Samuel Matthew (GSM)

Not all love is loud. Some of it lives quietly,in shared smiles, in familiar closeness, in the unspoken understanding between two souls who have chosen each other. ... See more Where Love Lives captures a moment that feels both ordinary and eternal. The embrace is gentle, yet full of meaning,a silent promise of presence, of support, of belonging. Their smiles are not just expressions of joy but reflections of comfort, trust, and a bond that has grown beyond words. The composition draws attention to connection over perfection. The closeness of their faces, the relaxed posture, and the soft harmony between them speak of a love that is not performative but deeply rooted. It is the kind of love that does not need to prove itself,it simply exists. The faint outline of the heart in the background is intentional, almost subtle, as if to suggest that love is not always something we see clearly, but something we feel. It surrounds them, holds them, and becomes part of their shared space. Through charcoal’s softness and depth, the work preserves a fleeting moment,transforming it into something permanent. It reminds us that love is not found in grand gestures alone, but in presence, in laughter, in the quiet decision to stay. Where Love Lives is not just about two people. It is about the universal human need to connect, to be seen, and to belong.

Title: The Weight of Influence

Artist: Samuel Matthew (GSM)

There are individuals whose presence extends beyond their physical form,whose impact lingers in spaces they have touched, voices they have shaped, and lives they have altered. The Weight of Influence is a reflection of such a figure. ... See more In this piece, the portrait emerges partially obscured, almost as if hidden behind layers of structure and system. This is not accidental. It speaks to the paradox of influence,the idea that the more powerful a figure becomes, the more they are filtered, interpreted, and sometimes confined by the very systems that amplify their voice. The visible fragments of the face carry a quiet intensity. There is strength, but also restraint. Recognition, yet distance. It suggests a life lived in the public eye, where identity is no longer entirely personal but shared, projected, and sometimes misunderstood. The mechanical elements,buttons, lights, and controlled interfaces serve as a metaphor for modern visibility and control. Influence, in this context, is no longer organic alone; it is structured, measured, and often mediated. The glowing indicators hint at constant attention, expectation, and the pressure to remain “on,” even when unseen. Yet beneath all of this, the human essence remains. The softness of the charcoal rendering reminds us that behind every influential figure is a person,one who feels, questions, and carries the unseen cost of being recognized. The Weight of Influence does not seek to define the subject but to question the nature of visibility itself. What does it mean to be seen by many, yet fully known by few?

It is time, memory, and humanity,held in one moment.

Title: At the Edge of Breath

Medium: Charcoal pencil on paper
Artist: Samuel Matthew (GSM)

There is a point where feeling becomes physical,where emotion rises from the chest and settles in the throat, heavy and unspoken. At the Edge of Breath lives in that exact moment. ... See more The head tilts back, not in ease, but in search of release. It is the posture of someone trying to make space inside themselves, trying to breathe through what cannot be easily expressed. The eyes withdraw from the world, not out of absence, but out of intensity,turned inward, where everything is happening at once. The tension in the neck holds the truth of the piece. It is where everything gathers. Words, pain, memories, questions,caught in a space too small to contain them, yet too heavy to let go. Nothing has been said, but everything is present. Around the figure, the dense, repeated textures feel like pressure,like thoughts that won’t quiet, like emotions that keep returning, again and again. And still, the figure remains. Not broken. Not free. Just there. at the edge. This work is not about collapse. It is about endurance. About the quiet, invisible moments where a person holds themselves together when no one else can see. Because sometimes, the hardest thing is not speaking… it is breathing through what you cannot say.

Title: Through One Eye

Medium: Charcoal pencil on paper
Artist: Samuel Matthew (GSM)

Through One Eye explores what it means to truly see when everything around you feels overwhelming ... See more Sometimes, clarity does not come from looking at the whole world at once. It comes from narrowing your focus,from choosing to see through one eye, one perspective, one moment at a time. In that quiet limitation, something deeper is revealed. This piece reflects an inward journey. A pause from external noise. A moment where the subject turns away from distraction and begins to confront what lies within,pain, truth, memory, and ultimately, understanding. There is a quiet strength in this act of seeing.
